PART A · LEARN

1 What these two paragraphs do

Paragraph 1 · IntroductionTells the reader what the visual shows. It is a paraphrase of the task: one or two sentences, about 20–35 words, no data and no opinion.
Paragraph 2 · OverviewTells the reader the big picture: the 2–4 most important trends, differences or stages. Begin with “Overall,” and use no figures.
Why it matters. The official public descriptors for Task Achievement ask for “a clear overview” at band 7, and only an attempted overview at band 6. At band 8, key features must be skilfully selected. Many teachers treat a missing overview as a ceiling of about band 5. Task 1 has no conclusion: the overview does that job.

Order: Introduction → Overview → Details 1 → Details 2. Put the overview in its own paragraph so the examiner finds it at once.

Four paraphrase techniques

TechniqueBeforeAfter
1 Synonymsthe percentage ofthe proportion / share of
2 Change the word classthe number of visitorshow many people visited
3 Change the structurethe percentage of households with a carhow common car ownership was
4 Reorder…between 1990 and 2010From 1990 to 2010, … / over a twenty-year period
Keep exactlynumbers and years · units · place names · key technical terms (e.g. CO₂) · the meaning
Nevercopy the task wording · add an opinion or reason · include data · write “This graph shows” without naming the chart type

Six task → introduction examples

TypeTask wordingParaphrase
Line graphThe graph shows the number of international students enrolled at a UK university between 2005 and 2020.The line graph illustrates how many overseas students were enrolled at a British university over a fifteen-year period, from 2005 to 2020.
Bar chartThe chart shows the percentage of adults who used public libraries in five countries in 2019.The bar chart compares what proportion of adults in five different countries made use of public libraries in 2019.
Pie chartsThe pie charts show the sources of electricity in a European country in 1990 and 2020.The pie charts compare how a European country generated its electricity in 1990 and in 2020.
TableThe table gives information about the average monthly spending of households in four countries on food, housing and transport.The table presents data on how much households in four countries spend each month, on average, on food, accommodation and transport.
ProcessThe diagram shows how paper is recycled.The diagram illustrates the stages involved in turning used paper into recycled paper.
MapThe maps show changes to a seaside town between 1990 and the present day.The two maps illustrate how a coastal town developed from 1990 to the present.
Tense: past simple for past years (“was enrolled”, “generated”); present simple for processes and for maps of today. Length: one sentence is enough; two at most.

3 The overview

Overall, [main trend / biggest difference / main stages]. [Second key feature or contrast].

Three ways to find the key features

  • Newspaper headline: what would the headline be if this were a news story?
  • Gun to head: you may mention only 2–4 things. Which?
  • Boss report: what must a busy boss know in ten seconds?

What to look for, by visual

VisualLook for
Line graphoverall direction of each line · highest / lowest · crossover points · stable vs volatile lines
Bar charttallest / shortest bars · groups that behave alike · contrast between groups · change over time if dynamic
Pie chartslargest / smallest segment · segments that grew or shrank · one dominant category
Tablehighest / lowest · biggest change · a clear pattern in rows or columns
Processhow many stages · where it starts and ends · linear or cyclical · main inputs and outputs
Mapbiggest changes (new / removed / replaced) · what stayed the same · overall direction of development

Openers and sentence patterns

Overall,In general,It is clear thatAs can be seen,The most striking feature is thatWhat stands out is
  • X rose in every country, while Y fell.
  • A was consistently higher than B.
  • X overtook Y to become the largest …
  • The process has N stages, beginning with … and ending with …
  • The most significant change was …, whereas … remained unchanged.
Dostart with “Overall,” · pick 2–4 features · compare · keep it to 2 sentences · make it a separate paragraph
Don’tput numbers in · give reasons or opinions · write “In conclusion” · claim more than the data shows · write only “there were many changes”

Weak → strong overviews

VisualWeakStrong
Line graphThe graph shows many changes in car ownership.Overall, car ownership rose in all four countries, with Japan showing the steepest growth, while the UK remained the highest throughout.
Bar chartMany people went to libraries in different countries.Overall, library use was far higher in the Nordic countries than elsewhere, and women used libraries more than men in every country.
Pie chartsIn 1990 coal was 50% and in 2020 it was 20%.Overall, coal went from being the dominant source to a minor one, while renewable energy showed the opposite trend and became the largest source by 2020.
TableHouseholds spend money on food, housing and transport.Overall, housing was the biggest expense in every country, and Country A spent the most on all three categories.
ProcessPaper is recycled in many steps.Overall, the process has six stages, beginning with the collection of used paper and ending with the delivery of new paper rolls.
MapThe town changed a lot.Overall, the town became far more developed, with the beach area turned into tourist facilities, while the old harbour remained unchanged.

5 Common mistakes and fixes

MistakeFix
Copying the task wordingRebuild the sentence with synonyms and a new structure.
Figures in the overviewMove them to the detail paragraphs; the overview describes trends only.
A vague overview (“many changes”)Name the changes: which rose, which fell, which is highest.
“In conclusion…” at the endTask 1 has no conclusion. Write “Overall,” as paragraph 2.
Reasons or opinions (“because people are richer”)Report only what the visual shows.
Only one feature coveredGive 2–4 features that together tell the whole story.
Unsupported claims (“the largest countries”)Claim only what the data shows.
Wrong degree word (“steadily” for a sharp fall, then slow)Match the adverb to the shape of the data.
Percent of what? (e.g. rate within a group vs share of all)Read the title and legend: say exactly what the whole is.
