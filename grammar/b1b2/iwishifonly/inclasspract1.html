I wish and If only: in-class practice

A rules recap, five checked exercises and two speaking tasks, including wishes and conditionals together.

Score 0 / 0Answered 0 / 0

1Recap: which form for which meaning?

Keep this table open while you work.

MeaningFormExample
A present situation you want to be differentwish + Past Simple / Past ContinuousI wish I lived by the sea. She wishes it weren’t raining.
A regret about the pastwish + Past PerfectI wish I had saved more money.
Annoyance at someone else’s habit, or at something that keeps happeningwish + would + infinitiveI wish the lift would work.
A formal request or desirewish + to + infinitiveI wish to speak to the manager.
If only works like wish but sounds stronger: If only I had a car. = I wish I had a car.
Compare: I hope it stops raining (it is possible). I wish it would stop raining (it is annoying, and we can’t control it).
Link to conditionals: If only I had left earlier. = If I had left earlier, I wouldn’t have missed the train.
Avoid: wish + will · I wish I would (use could) · wish + would have + V3 (use had + V3).

3Your regrets and wishes

Put the verb into the correct form. Afterwards, read each sentence aloud and tell your partner whether you feel the same: “So do I.” / “Not me.”

4Complete the conversation

Choose the missing phrase for each gap. Each phrase is used once.

5Turn the situation into a wish

Complete the sentences with the correct words and forms. Use I wish or If only. Example: My desk is too small. → I wish I had a bigger desk.

6Wish or conditional?

For each situation, complete a wish sentence and a conditional sentence about the same idea.

7Speaking: sentence starters

Choose one small regret and one persistent irritation. Speak for about thirty seconds on each, honestly and briefly. Your partner asks one follow-up question after each answer.

Complete these starters aloud with I wish or If only:

  • I wish I were more … (a quality you would like to have)
  • If only I had asked … before …
  • I wish my family / friends / colleagues would stop … (something that annoys you)
  • If only my city had …
  • I wish I could … (a skill you don’t have yet)
  • I wish to … (a formal request you might make in a letter or at a meeting)
