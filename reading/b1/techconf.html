My First International Tech Conference

Posted by Igor Marchenko, 14 September

It's over now! I've been thinking about the last few days ever since I got back home, and I want to write everything down before I forget the details. It was my first business trip abroad as a project manager, and it changed the way I think about international work. Here are the moments that stayed with me the most.

The Flight and the Delay

Everything started badly. My flight was at six o'clock in the morning, so I arrived at the airport at four. Unfortunately, there was a technical problem with the plane, and we waited in the departure lounge for almost three hours. I was very nervous because my first meeting was scheduled for eleven o'clock, and I didn't want to miss it. In the end, we landed in Berlin at half past ten, and I ran through the whole airport with my laptop bag on my back!

The Opening Keynote

Despite the delay, I got to the conference venue just on time. The opening keynote began at eleven, in a huge hall with hundreds of people from different countries. The main speaker talked about the future of automation for almost an hour, and I understood much more than I expected. I took a lot of notes on my phone and even asked a question at the end — my first question in English in front of so many strangers!

Meeting the Clients

On the second day, I finally met our clients from the Netherlands and Poland face to face. We had only spoken on video calls before, so it felt strange to shake hands with them in real life. We sat at a small table in the exhibition area and talked about the new project for almost two hours. They spoke slowly and patiently with me, and by the end of the conversation, I felt much more confident about my English.

The Presentation That Went Wrong

On the third day, I gave a short presentation about our team's work. Everything was fine until my laptop suddenly froze in the middle of a slide! I stood there in silence for a few seconds, and then one of the Dutch clients laughed kindly and said, "Welcome to conference life." Everybody smiled, and I finished the presentation from memory. It wasn't perfect, but people still came to talk to me afterwards.

The City in the Evening

In the evenings, our small group walked around the city together. We ate dinner at a different restaurant every night and talked about work, football and our families. On the last evening, we stood by the river until midnight, and I realised that I understood almost every joke my colleagues made — something that had never happened to me before this trip.

Coming Home

I flew back home on Sunday afternoon, tired but happy. I made new business contacts, I survived a technical disaster in front of an audience, and I spoke English for four days without translating everything in my head first. Next time, I won't be so nervous — and I definitely won't arrive at the airport two hours too early again!
