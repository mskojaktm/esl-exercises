1Rules review

Present simple

AffirmativeI / you / we / they work.   He / she / it works.
NegativeI / you / we / they don't work.   He / she / it doesn't work.
QuestionDo you work?   Does she work?
Short answersYes, I do. / No, I don't.   Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

We use it for:

  • habits and routines — I get up at seven every day.
  • permanent situations — My uncle works in a bank.
  • general and scientific facts — Water boils at 100°C.
Time expressions: always, usually, normally, often, sometimes, rarely, never, once / twice / three times a week, every day, on Mondays

Spelling: he / she / it

  • most verbs: + -s — play → plays, read → reads
  • after -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o: + -es — fix → fixes, go → goes, finish → finishes
  • consonant + y: y → -ies — try → tries, study → studies (but play → plays)
  • irregular: have → has

Present continuous

Affirmativesubject + am / are / is + verb-ing — They're playing football.
Negativesubject + am not / aren't / isn't + verb-ing — He isn't listening.
QuestionAm / Are / Is + subject + verb-ing? — Is it raining?
Short answersYes, I am. / No, I'm not.   Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

We use it for:

  • actions happening now — Be quiet! The baby is sleeping.
  • temporary actions — My brother is working in Moscow this month.
Time expressions: now, right now, at the moment, today, this week

Spelling: -ing

  • most verbs: + -ing — read → reading, play → playing
  • verb ends in -e: drop the e — make → making, ride → riding
  • verb ends in -ie: ie → -ying — die → dying, tie → tying
  • one syllable, consonant + vowel + consonant: double the last letter — run → running, swim → swimming

State verbs

Some verbs describe states, not actions. We don't usually use them in the continuous form:

believe, hate, have (= possess), hear, know, love, mean, need, prefer, see, seem, think (= have an opinion), understand, want
  • I know the answer.   ✗ I'm knowing the answer.
  • She has two brothers. — but: She's having lunch. (have = eat, an action)
  • I think it's a good film. (opinion) — but: I'm thinking about my holiday. (action)

Articles: a / an, the, no article

a / an

  • singular countable nouns, when we mention something for the first time or say it is one of many — I've got a cat. It's a black cat.
  • jobs — My aunt is a nurse.
  • a before a consonant sound, an before a vowel sound — a book, an apple, an hour

the

  • something we mentioned before — I bought a cake. The cake was delicious.
  • specific things or people — The teacher we have this year is great.
  • unique things — the moon, the internet, the president

no article ( – )

  • plural or uncountable nouns when we talk about people or things in general — Dogs are friendly. Music makes me happy.
