Part A. The rules

  • -body and -one mean exactly the same: somebody = someone.
  • no one is written as two words. nobody is one word.
  • People → -body / -one. Things and ideas → -thing. Places → -where.

some: что-то, кто-то, где-то · any: что-нибудь, кто-нибудь, где-нибудь · no: ничего, никто, нигде · every: всё, все, везде.

2Which one do I need? Four questions

  1. Is there already a negative word (not, n't, never, hardly, without)?
    → use any-: I didn't see anybody.
  2. Is it a question?
    → a neutral question uses any-: Did you see anybody?
    → an offer or request uses some-: Would you like something to drink?
  3. Do you want a negative idea, but a positive verb?
    → use no-: I saw nobody. = Nobody came.
  4. A positive statement.
    → a person / thing / place we don't know or don't name: some-: Somebody called.
    → it doesn't matter which one (free choice): any-: Anybody can join.

3some- : positive sentences, offers and requests

Positive statements — the person, thing or place exists, but we don't say exactly which.

  • Somebody left a message for you.
  • I've left my phone somewhere in the office.
  • There's something wrong with the server.

Questions that are offers or requests — we expect the answer “yes”.

  • Would you like something to drink?
  • Could you give me something to write with?
  • Can someone help me with this?

some- в утверждениях и в вежливых предложениях / просьбах (ожидаем ответ «да»).

4any- : negatives, questions, “it doesn't matter”

After a negative word

  • I don't know anything about it.
  • We haven't been anywhere this year.
  • She never tells anybody.
  • He left without saying anything.
  • There's hardly anything left.

In neutral questions and after if

  • Is there anything interesting on TV?
  • Did you go anywhere at the weekend?
  • If anyone calls, say I'm in a meeting.
  • If you need anything, ask me.

Free choice (positive sentence): any- means “it doesn't matter who / what / where”.

  • Anyone can learn English if they practise.
  • You can sit anywhere you like.
  • Ask me anything!

any-: в вопросах и после отрицания = «-нибудь»; в утверждении = «любой, кто угодно, где угодно».

5no- : a negative meaning with a positive verb

  • Nobody knows the answer.
  • There is nothing in the box.
  • I have nowhere to go tonight.
  • “Who called?” — “No one.” “What did you say?” — “Nothing.”
One negative per clause! English never uses two negatives together.
  • ✗ I don't know nothing.
  • ✓ I know nothing.  or  ✓ I don't know anything.

Осторожно: по-русски два отрицания («Я ничего не знаю»), по-английски — только одно.

6every- : all

  • Everybody is here. Let's start.
  • Everything was clean and the staff were friendly.
  • I've looked everywhere, but I can't find my passport.

The verb is singular even though the meaning is “all”: Everyone has a laptop.

every-: все, всё, везде. Глагол всегда в единственном числе: Everybody is…

7Grammar details (B2 level)

Singular verb

  • Nobody knows. Something is wrong.

“They / their” for a person

  • Someone left their umbrella here.
  • If anybody calls, tell them I'm busy.

Если пол неизвестен, используем they / them / their.

Adjective goes AFTER the word

  • something interesting (✗ interesting something)
  • nothing special, anyone new, somewhere quiet

to + verb after the word

  • something to eat, nothing to do
  • somewhere to stay, anyone to talk to

+ else = “other / more”

  • Do you need anything else?  ·  Let's ask someone else.  ·  It's someone else's laptop.

No “to” and no “of”

  • We went somewhere nice. (✗ went to somewhere)
  • None of us knows. / One of us knows. (✗ nobody of us, someone of us)
